Loss-prevention method and costs

Loss prevention now estimates when settlement at zero becomes more likely than winning, using current bid and remaining time within each original entry category and contract series. The table shows estimated 50% risk boundary ranges; tap a cell for observed rates, 95% uncertainty intervals and event counts. Estimates appear as soon as quotes and settled outcomes exist: no seven-day wait and no complete-future-path requirement. Auto chooses an available checkpoint at or before half the original entry time. Sparse, conflicting or missing price ranges remain unresolved. A risk boundary is not a sale recommendation. Sale-versus-hold estimates are shown separately with a hypothetical 2¢ exit cost. Risk estimates use all saved history in both views; latest P/L stays latest-only. Original P/L, ROI and sample counts remain unchanged because descriptive risk is not a validated exit backtest. Event representatives reduce correlated outcomes; observations across table rows still reuse events. Times are relative to saved contract close, which may differ from event end or settlement.

What am I looking at?

The large number in each square is the average simulated profit or loss per contract. For example, +4.2¢ means that group gained an average of 4.2 cents per hypothetically filled contract under the displayed model and cost assumptions. A loss of −8¢ means it lost an average of eight cents.

The loss-prevention switch adds current-price risk boundaries to this table. Tap a square for loss probabilities by current bid and remaining time. Risk estimates do not change historical returns or claim that an early sale was filled. Comparing rows is exploratory: repeated observations of the same contracts are not independent trades.
