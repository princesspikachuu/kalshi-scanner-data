Loss-prevention method and costs

Both switch positions retain the full saved dataset. The on view adds only the measured exit-minus-hold difference for covered validation positions; positions without exit evidence remain at the holding baseline. This is a partially modeled overlay, not a full-coverage strategy backtest. Each cell shows the downside sale price; tap for the matched hold result, improvement, and contract-family rules. Thresholds are tested in 5¢ steps using five earlier training days and at least two later validation days. The best expected-outcome candidate must pass the evidence checks; an uncertain candidate is not replaced with a smaller salvage threshold. Training rows and missing paths are excluded from exit testing, but remain in the full-table holding baseline. No supported threshold means hold. Entry fees remain excluded consistently with the original table; each modeled early sale adds a 2¢ cost stress, not verified market fees. Minute-close bids assume fills without depth, latency or slippage replay.

What am I looking at?

The large number in each square is the average simulated profit or loss per contract. For example, +4.2¢ means that group gained an average of 4.2 cents per hypothetically filled contract under the displayed model and cost assumptions. A loss of −8¢ means it lost an average of eight cents.

When price-path data is available, the switch recalculates this table using a matched sample and the stated fee stress. Tap a populated square for its exit thresholds, hold comparison, ROI, and coverage. Comparing rows is exploratory: repeated observations of the same contracts are not independent trades.
